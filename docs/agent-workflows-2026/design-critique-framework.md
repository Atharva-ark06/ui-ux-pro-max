# Structured Design Critique Framework

A useful critique identifies the problem, its impact, and a practical next step instead of relying on vague aesthetic judgments.

## Review dimensions

1. Hierarchy: is the primary task obvious?
2. Consistency: are spacing, typography, and components predictable?
3. Accessibility: can people perceive and operate the UI?
4. Feedback: are loading, success, and error states clear?
5. Responsiveness: does the layout adapt to narrow viewports?
6. Content: are labels and instructions specific and understandable?
7. Performance: do large images, animation, or effects slow the experience?

## Finding format

- Observation: what is visible or reproducible?
- Impact: who is affected and how?
- Evidence: frame, screenshot, interaction, or test result.
- Recommendation: the smallest useful improvement.
- Priority: user impact and urgency.

## Guardrails

Separate verified observations from assumptions. Do not recommend visual changes that conflict with an established design system without explaining why. Validate improvements with a prototype, usability check, or appropriate test.
