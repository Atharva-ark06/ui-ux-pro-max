# Data Visualization
1. State the question the chart should answer.
2. Choose an encoding appropriate to the comparison: position for precise comparison, lines for trends, and bars for category magnitudes.
3. Label units, time ranges, sources, and meaningful baselines.
4. Avoid misleading axes, unnecessary 3D effects, and color palettes that obscure differences.
5. Provide accessible labels and a text/table alternative for important values.
6. Distinguish missing, zero, and unavailable data.
7. Handle loading, empty, error, and outlier states.
8. Verify the chart remains interpretable in grayscale and at narrow widths.
